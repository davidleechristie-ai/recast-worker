Compare Heat Pump Quotes Before You Pay a Deposit

Two heat-pump quotes can look similar until you check what each price includes. One may cover radiator changes and electrical work. Another may leave those costs subject to survey. Before choosing, understand what is confirmed, what is missing and what could change.

Compare the work included

If something is not mentioned, ask. Silence does not confirm that it is included.

  • Heat pump, controls and installation.
  • Hot-water cylinder and associated work.
  • Radiator, pipework and insulation changes.
  • Electrical work, equipment removal and making good.
  • Commissioning, controls demonstration and handover.

Look for the design evidence

These details help explain how the proposal is intended to heat your home.

  • Room-by-room heat-loss calculation.
  • Indoor and outdoor design temperatures.
  • Proposed heating flow temperature.
  • Radiator or emitter assessment.
  • Explanation of the selected heat-pump model.

Compare the actual amount payable

Compare the same basis across every quote. Ask what happens if the assumed funding is not available.

  • Total installation price before funding.
  • Any grant or discount shown separately.
  • Your contribution.
  • Provisional allowances and optional extras.
  • Work you must arrange separately.

Clarify assumptions and exclusions

These phrases are points to clarify, rather than automatic reasons to reject a quote. Ask for written answers and keep them with the proposal.

  • Subject to survey.
  • Electrical upgrades excluded.
  • Radiator changes if required.
  • Allowance included.
  • Customer to arrange.

Check aftercare and payment stages

Read the payment and cancellation terms before committing.

  • Who handles equipment faults and installation defects?
  • What servicing conditions apply to warranties?
  • What does the deposit secure?
  • When are further payments due?
  • What happens if the design, price or timetable changes?

A lower headline price may cover less

Illustrative example, not a price benchmark: Quote A is £12,400 including electrical work. Quote B is £11,900 excluding it. For this example only, both amounts include VAT and neither has funding deducted. No equipment, property or design equivalence is assumed.

The £500 headline difference is known. The difference for equivalent work is not. An unpriced exclusion is an unknown cost, not £0; do not turn it into a saving. Ask whether electrical work means the same thing in both proposals, who will arrange it, and for a written price covering it.

Put every proposal on the same basis

  1. Record the quoted installation amount, whether VAT is included, and the quote date or version.
  2. List required work outside that amount. Keep unpriced items marked “unknown”; keep optional upgrades separate from work needed for the proposed installation.
  3. Show any funding separately. If a quote already deducts a grant, do not deduct it again. Confirm the funding assumptions rather than treating an illustration as an eligibility decision.
  4. Check the equipment and design evidence as well as scope. Matching totals do not establish matching output, comfort or running costs.
  5. Compare again using the written revisions. Keep the earlier proposals so you can see which work or assumptions changed.

Use the scope record to identify unresolved items, and the design evidence guide for the technical questions. This comparison does not establish a fair price or select equipment.

Frequently asked questions

Should I choose the cheapest heat-pump quote?

Compare the documented scope and design evidence first. A lower price may reflect different equipment, less work or unresolved allowances.

Can I check just one quote?

Yes. Start by identifying gaps in one proposal before requesting another.

Does a missing item mean the installer has made a mistake?

Not necessarily. It may be in a separate document or awaiting a survey. Ask for written clarification.

Will Home Quote Check choose an installer for me?

No. It helps you understand the proposals. The decision remains yours.
