Check the rating conditions, not just the watts

A radiator’s output depends on its size and type, water temperatures, water flow and room temperature. A catalogue figure at ΔT50 is not its output at every heat-pump design condition. Ask for the manufacturer’s output data for the actual radiator at the temperatures used in your design.

What does radiator ΔT mean?

Here, radiator ΔT means the mean water temperature minus the room temperature. It is different from the temperature drop between flow and return.

Illustrative temperatures only: with flow at 45°C, return at 35°C and a room at 20°C, the mean water temperature is (45 + 35) ÷ 2 = 40°C. The radiator-to-room difference is 40 − 20 = 20°C (ΔT20). The flow-to-return drop is 10°C.

This arithmetic does not give a radiator’s wattage, recommend these design temperatures or prove that it can heat a room. Output must come from the relevant manufacturer data or correction method; it is not a simple proportional conversion from ΔT50.

Request a room-by-room emitter record

For each room, ask the installer to put these items together so the proposed changes can be understood:

  1. Heat loss and room temperature: the calculated heat requirement in watts and the indoor temperature used, with the calculation’s outdoor design condition.
  2. Radiator identity: whether it stays or changes, its type and dimensions, and the proposed replacement model where applicable.
  3. Design water temperatures: the flow and return temperatures used for the output check, rather than flow temperature alone.
  4. Output evidence: the radiator output in watts at those conditions, plus the manufacturer data or correction method used.
  5. Scope and unresolved items: which changes are included in the price, any survey assumptions and who will confirm missing evidence before commitment.

For the wider design evidence, see our heat-loss and flow-temperature guide. A total property heat loss alone does not show whether an individual room has sufficient emitter output.

If the quote only says “radiators suitable”

Keep suitability unverified until the supporting record is available. A warm room on the current boiler settings or a catalogue wattage without its rating condition does not establish output at the proposed heat-pump conditions.

“Please send the room-by-room radiator schedule showing the heat loss, design room/flow/return temperatures, existing or replacement radiator details, and output at those conditions. Please also identify which upgrades are priced and which items remain subject to survey.”

When comparing proposals, check differences in emitter scope alongside the heat-pump quote comparison; a lower headline price may cover different work.

Common quote questions

What should I ask an installer before accepting a heat pump quote?

Ask for the room-by-room heat-loss calculation, design flow temperature, radiator or emitter schedule, electrical scope, exclusions, provisional items, commissioning information and warranty details.

Should radiator changes be listed in a heat pump quote?

Yes. A clear proposal should identify which radiators or emitters need changing and the design assumptions used to justify those changes.
